ManaBox vs Moxfield

Short answer: ManaBox is a phone app built around scanning paper cards into a collection. Moxfield is a website built around building and sharing decks. They overlap on collection tracking and deck lists, but each is clearly stronger at one job, and plenty of players use both.

Pick ManaBox if

  • You have a box of paper cards and want them in a collection fast. Pointing a phone at each card is quicker than typing.
  • You want to check a price or a collection count at a store, with no connection needed.
  • You mostly use your phone and want decks, trades and prices in one app.

Pick Moxfield if

  • Building decks at a computer is your main use. Its editor, search filters and playtester are what most reviews praise.
  • You want to share a decklist link that anyone in your playgroup or on a forum can open without installing anything.
  • You want to browse other people's decks for ideas.

Using both

A common setup is scanning with ManaBox and building with Moxfield. ManaBox exports your whole collection as CSV, including the binder or list name of each card, and Moxfield imports collection CSV files. ManaBox can also import a deck straight from a Moxfield link. Check what the import keeps: binder names are the detail most likely to be lost along the way.

Common questions

Does Moxfield have a card scanner?

No. Third-party comparisons agree that Moxfield has no camera scanning, so paper cards go in by search, a text list or a CSV, often one exported from a scanner app such as ManaBox.

Is ManaBox a deck builder?

It can save decks, show charts and simulate games, but reviewers describe it as a collection app first. Most players who care about deck building also use a website.

Can I use ManaBox on a computer?

ManaBox's own site lists iOS and Android apps only. We did not find a first-party web version.

Which is cheaper?

Both are free to use. Each has an optional paid or supporter tier, so the cost depends on whether you want the extras.
